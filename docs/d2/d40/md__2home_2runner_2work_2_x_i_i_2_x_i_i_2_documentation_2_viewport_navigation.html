Viewport navigation gizmo

Scene viewports and 3D asset previews show an orientation widget in their upper-right corner. Toggle Viewport Navigation Gizmo in the viewport toolbar to hide or restore it independently in each view. The setting belongs to that view configuration for its lifetime; it is not a global preference. Views smaller than 128 logical pixels on either side hide the widget until they have room again. Stereo cameras are excluded.

Mesh, animated mesh, animation clip and skeleton previews retain a far clipping distance of at least 1,000 metres so framing a small asset does not cut off the surrounding grid. Their clipping range follows the current camera distance and asset bounds on each update, including wheel zoom, free movement and later bounds replies. The near plane remains scaled to the asset for close inspection. Fixed material/texture previews and orthographic clipping ranges retain their configured values.

  • Red, green and blue identify world X, Y and Z. Positive axes have labels; negative axes have smaller hollow markers. Rear axes are dimmed and drawn before front axes.
  • Click a marker to view the scene from that side. Click the aligned marker again to flip to the opposite side. The camera animates about its existing orbit target without changing its distance, field of view or orthographic dimension.
  • Drag anywhere inside the circular widget to orbit. Movement continues while the pointer is captured outside the widget. Escape, focus loss or disabling the widget ends the interaction. A click released outside its original marker does not snap.
  • Orthographic cameras retain orthographic projection when snapping or orbiting. Axis snaps update the existing front/right/top view classification. The camera's configured navigation up direction determines upright views; when viewing along that up axis, its configured forward direction supplies the screen up direction (XII defaults: Z-up, +X at the top of a top view).

xiiEditorViewportNavigationGizmo owns input only. xiiQtEngineViewWidget supplies the existing interpolation clock and per-view routing, while the scene and preview views delegate orbiting to their existing camera contexts. Quaternion interpolation of the complete camera basis avoids singular look-at vectors and maintains the pivot throughout the animation. The controllers share a quaternion orbit operation that also works at exact poles.

xiiViewportNavigationGizmo shares logical-pixel layout, camera-basis projection, depth sorting and marker hit testing between editor and engine processes. The redraw message carries visibility and hover state. Engine drawing uses the owning view's debug context, existing screen-space shaders, text and pooled upload path. Filled circles have a one-physical-pixel alpha fringe. Overlay lines and circles disable depth testing and writes; no new render pass or gizmo-specific GPU resource is introduced. Device pixel ratio scales drawing once, while Qt input remains in logical pixels.

Regression coverage includes arbitrary and rolled camera orientations, six directions and flips, intermediate orbit invariants, configured Y-up navigation, pole continuity, mouse capture, outside input, disabling, resizing, independent scene/preview views, and GPU overlay readback at 100%, 150% and 200% scale. GPU cases verify view isolation, scene-depth bypass, translation independence, hover differences and fractional edge coverage. On Windows, hidden native viewport captures exercise the production editor-to-engine messages, rendering and enabled/disabled bounds at each Qt scale factor. Supported backends use the same GAL/debug rendering path; backend and manual acceptance results are recorded in the pull request.
